Backup · tough questions

What we might be asked (1 of 2)

1Does credit cause adoption?
We can't claim that. With observational data we measure association: we compare similar stores, but there may be factors we don't see, such as the owner's management. We tried an instrument and fixed effects, and neither allowed us to identify causality.
2Couldn't it be the other way around?
It's plausible: a store with digital payments has recorded sales and finds it easier to get bank credit. That's why we talk about association and not about the effect of credit.
3Why Logit and Probit and not a linear model?
The variable is yes or no. The linear model can predict probabilities below 0 or above 100%; Logit and Probit can't. We estimated it anyway as a check, and it gives the same sign and significance.
4Does the result depend on the controls?
No. The credit effect stays between 11 and 12 points with different controls, and it holds with Probit, the linear model, and ordered and multinomial logit.

What we might be asked (2 of 2)

5Why did you group basic and moderate?
Because it's a self-classification and the boundary between the two is blurry for whoever answers. Grouping them reduces measurement error, and the margin we care about is reaching the high level.
6Can self-reported technology be trusted?
It's a limitation: there may be over- or under-reporting. We narrowed it with levels defined by concrete examples (QR, point of sale, inventory) and we state it explicitly.
7What about the stores that stopped responding?
Participation fell month by month, so the 190 stores in the panel aren't a random sample of the 998. That's why the analysis over time is exploratory and supports no central conclusion.
8Can it be generalized?
Not directly. The sample wasn't designed to be representative: contest participants surveyed it store by store, in areas of Greater Buenos Aires. Generalizing requires replicating it in other cities.
