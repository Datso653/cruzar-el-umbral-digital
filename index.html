Is having credit enough to cross the digital threshold?

Credit, education and life cycle in the technology adoption of nano-stores in Greater Buenos Aires

Juan Da Torre UBA Gina Marrazzo UBA Sofía Gálvez UNSAM
Hi, we are team Green Thunder. We bring you one question: is having credit enough for a neighborhood store to go fully digital?
The problem

Almost all got in.
Few made it.

Of every 100 stores in Greater Buenos Aires, 97 use some digital tool. Only 19 run most of their processes digitally.

3 with no tools at all 78 entered the digital world 19 reached high adoption
Green Thunder · MIT LIFT Lab · The problem
Of every 100 stores we surveyed, 97 already use some digital tool, but only 19 run most of their processes digitally. Getting in is no longer the challenge; getting there is.
The question

What sets those 19 apart?

Our hypothesis
If a nano-store has access to credit, then it reaches a higher level of technology adoption.
Adoption can be understood as two different thresholds: entering the digital world and reaching high adoption.
Green Thunder · MIT LIFT Lab · The question
Our question: which factors are associated with reaching high technology adoption? We started from a hypothesis, credit, and from an idea that organizes everything: adoption can be understood as two different thresholds.

How we studied it · the framework

Adopting isn't one leap: it's two

Green Thunder · MIT LIFT Lab · The framework
That's why we separate two thresholds: entering the digital world, which 97% have already crossed, and reaching high adoption, where the difference lies. Each one has its own model.

Green Thunder · MIT LIFT Lab · The method
We studied nano-stores in Greater Buenos Aires: their technology adoption and their access to credit. We used Logit and Probit because it's a yes-or-no question, and we read them as marginal effects: how many points the probability goes up. One clear limit: we talk about association, not cause.
Finding · 1

Bank credit is associated with the leap,
not with the entry

+11 pp

Dot: average marginal effect. Line: 95% confidence interval. If the line crosses zero, we can't tell the effect apart from zero. n = 814.

Green Thunder · MIT LIFT Lab · Finding 1
This is the central finding. To reach high adoption, bank credit is associated with 11 more points of probability, and its interval doesn't touch zero. At the entry, the interval crosses zero. Careful: saying credit "goes along with" the leap means they go together, not that it causes it.

Green Thunder · MIT LIFT Lab · Finding 2
And it's not just any money: with all sources in the same equation, only bank credit is associated with high adoption. Supplier credit funds day-to-day operations; bank credit, longer-term investments.
Finding · 3

Adoption is also related
to other characteristics of the business

Not explained by: crime, competition or inflation.  ·  Stores with more employees also adopt more.
Green Thunder · MIT LIFT Lab · Finding 3
Credit isn't alone: more education of the owner adds up, and older stores adopt less, about 5 points less every ten years. The environment (crime, competition, inflation) doesn't explain adoption.
Statistical honesty

What we tried that didn't work

✕
Sociodemographic Endowment Index
Summarizing education, age, gender and premises ownership in a single index. Its parts didn't measure anything in common.
✕
Neighbors' credit as an instrument
Where a store sets up isn't random: location is endogenous.
✕
Store fixed effects
In six months, only 2 stores changed both credit and technology at the same time.

Showing what didn't work is also part of the result.

Green Thunder · MIT LIFT Lab · What we ruled out
We also show what didn't work: a sociodemographic index whose parts didn't measure anything in common, an instrument we ruled out because location is endogenous, and fixed effects without enough variation.
Implication

Support in stages, not one size fits all

Fund the expansionCredit to move from intermediate to high: equipment and management software.
Credit plus trainingThe owner's education adds up alongside credit.
Look at established storesThe businesses with more years are the ones falling furthest behind.
Green Thunder · MIT LIFT Lab · Implication
This means designing support in stages: at the entry, training and hands-on support; in the step up to high adoption, bank credit with training. And don't forget the stores with more years.
Conclusion
Is having credit enough to cross the digital threshold?
Not necessarily.
But the type of credit matters.

Among nano-stores in Greater Buenos Aires, bank credit is associated with a higher probability of reaching high technology adoption.

Why does it matter?Promoting digitalization isn't only about making credit easier to get: it also matters to understand what financing stores need to move toward deeper adoption.

Green Thunder · MIT LIFT Lab · Conclusion
Back to the question: is having credit enough? Not necessarily, but the type of credit matters: bank credit is associated with 11 more points. Promoting digitalization isn't just handing out credit; it's understanding what financing each store needs. Always as an association, not a cause.
